- type: commitments
  id: values
  eyebrow: Shared values
  title: What we stand for
  intro: Six values that guide how the members work, with each other and with the people they serve.
  variant: detail
  items:
  - title: Independence and neutrality
    icon: compass
    body: Services that are impartial and guided only by humanitarian needs, free from political, commercial or institutional influence.
    detail: 'Our organizations are committed to delivering services that are independent, impartial, and guided solely by humanitarian needs and principles. All products, analyses, and technical support are developed free from political, commercial, or institutional influence. By maintaining neutrality and independence, our organizations can strengthen trust among governments, humanitarian actors, donors, and affected populations, ensuring that data and evidence remain credible and universally accessible tools for decision-making.


      Each member organization, and each new member, certifies its internal code of conduct and policies enforce these principles.'
  - title: Collaboration over competition
    icon: link
    body: Cooperation, knowledge sharing and joint problem-solving come before competing for visibility or resources.
    detail: 'Our organizations recognize that collective impact is greater than individual organizational success. Members actively prioritize cooperation, knowledge sharing, and joint problem-solving over competition for visibility, resources, or influence. By leveraging each organization''s comparative advantage, the HDA can create greater value for humanitarian stakeholders while reducing fragmentation and duplication across the humanitarian data ecosystem.


      While we acknowledge the need for maintaining a certain level of competition to avoid monopolies and foster innovation and efficiency, we seek to remove it where it is not necessary or useful in the non-profit sector (thus mixing cooperation and competition or “coopetition”). The HDA doesn’t aim to expand infinitely or consolidate the whole humanitarian data civil society ecosystem: expansion would be in view of better serving humanitarians and all those seeking improved humanitarian outcomes, from local to global. Adding new members will aim at bringing new capabilities or approaches and innovative ways of achieving outcomes, distinct or complementary from the existing members.


      The HDA will pursue partnerships and collaborations with other members of the humanitarian data ecosystem: United Nations agencies, public authorities, international and national NGOs, but also technical organizations not members of the HDA. The HDA will thus maintain a “coopetition” framework with external organizations, always with the goal of maximizing the efficiency of the ecosystem.'
  - title: Equity
    icon: scale
    body: Data and services that serve the people most often left out, with local actors able to take part, lead and benefit.
    detail: The HDA is committed to ensuring that data, services, and information products contribute to equitable humanitarian action. Particular attention is given to the needs of vulnerable, marginalized, and underrepresented populations whose voices are often absent from decision-making processes. Equity also extends to the HDA’s model and collaboration, and overall engagement with local actors, ensuring that national and sub-national stakeholders have meaningful opportunities to participate, lead, and benefit from the services provided.
  - title: Open data and responsible data stewardship
    icon: shield
    body: Data as open as possible and as protected as necessary.
    detail: 'The HDA believes that data should be as open as possible and as protected as necessary. Members promote the responsible sharing and use of data to maximize collective benefit while safeguarding privacy, security, and ethical considerations. This balance between openness and protection ensures that information can support effective humanitarian action without exposing individuals or communities to harm. Openness also extends to open knowledge and sharing of resources and skills, promoting a data sharing culture and practices in the humanitarian sector.


      Each member organization, and each new member, certifies its data protection / responsible / ethical data policies enforce these principles.'
  - title: Trust and transparency
    icon: eye
    body: Open decision-making, resource allocation and reporting, so partners and stakeholders can rely on us.
    detail: Trust is the foundation of effective collaboration. The HDA commits to transparency in decision-making, resource allocation, service delivery, and performance measurement. Members openly share information, opportunities, risks, and challenges, fostering an environment of mutual respect and accountability. Through transparency, the partnership strengthens confidence among stakeholders and creates the conditions necessary for long-term collaboration and impact.
  - title: Common cause
    icon: heart-handshake
    body: Outcomes, lessons and challenges belong to the Alliance as a whole, not to any one member.
    detail: The partnership embraces a communal approach to our work together. Outcomes, achievements, lessons learned, and challenges belong to the HDA as a whole rather than to individual organizations. This approach reinforces mutual accountability, encourages long-term commitment, and promotes a culture in which all members contribute actively to the sustainability and effectiveness of the HDA.
- type: commitments
  id: principles
  eyebrow: Operating principles
  title: How we work
  intro: Six principles for how the Alliance designs and delivers its services.
  variant: detail
  items:
  - title: Interoperability by design
    icon: puzzle
    body: Services, systems and data that work together, with common standards and open source wherever it fits.
    detail: The HDA is committed to designing services, systems, workflows, and products that work seamlessly together. Technical interoperability, common standards, shared methodologies, and compatible data architectures enable information to flow efficiently across organizations and operational contexts. This includes open source software whenever it is the most relevant solution for end-users. By embedding interoperability from the outset, the partnership creates a coherent service ecosystem that maximizes efficiency and value for end users.
  - title: Complementarity of mission statements
    icon: stack
    body: Each member focuses on what it does best and supports the strengths of the others.
    detail: Each organization contributes distinct expertise, capabilities, and operational strengths that collectively create a more comprehensive service offering. The partnership is founded on the principle that organizations should focus on their comparative advantages while supporting the strengths of others. This approach reduces duplication, strengthens service quality, and ensures that stakeholders benefit from the best capabilities available across the HDA.
  - title: Innovate with purpose
    icon: bulb
    body: New technology only where it solves a real humanitarian problem. Technology alone is never the solution.
    detail: Innovation is pursued not as an end in itself, but as a means of solving real humanitarian challenges. The HDA actively explores new technologies, methodologies, and approaches – including geospatial technologies, artificial intelligence, data science, and community-driven mapping – while ensuring that innovations remain practical, ethical, scalable, and responsive to operational needs. Every innovation should contribute to improved decision-making and better humanitarian outcomes, technology alone is never the solution.
  - title: Demand and needs-driven services
    icon: target
    body: Services shaped by what humanitarian actors and affected people need, not by our own priorities.
    detail: The HDA exists to serve the needs of humanitarian actors, governments, and affected populations. Services are designed and delivered in response to identified operational demands rather than organizational priorities. By remaining responsive to user requirements and evolving contexts, the HDA ensures that its support remains relevant, timely, and impactful.
  - title: Accountability towards people, communities and autonomy of end users
    icon: people
    body: People always come before data. We work with local actors and build capacity that lasts without us.
    detail: 'Each of our organizations is accountable to its end users, partners, donors, and affected populations. People always come before data. The HDA’s activities will be respectful to the rights of individuals and communities. It will take into account the best interests of the community and individuals, before adding data solutions.


      Whenever possible, activities are undertaken in collaboration with local individuals, communities, and organizations (i.e. NGO Forums, NNGOs, Local or National Governments) and in a respectful manner at all times to support long term uptake of data in all contexts. This includes collaboration with local and national technical specialists and their ecosystem, and promoting and supporting local innovations.


      The activities will build sustainable and autonomous local capacity and will ensure access to open tools and technology, and provide access to training for individuals and communities they work with. Members acknowledge that a true localization approach can mean reducing our power footprint and eventually our activities, targeting to make our support unnecessary eventually.


      Members are committed to delivering high-quality services, measuring performance, and continuously improving based on evidence and feedback. Accountability mechanisms ensure that resources are used effectively, commitments are honored, and services contribute meaningfully to humanitarian decision-making and outcomes. This will include anticipating and mitigating possible harms generated by activities by building efficient and context-specific safeguards.'
  - title: Fit for purpose
    icon: tool
    body: 'No single solution fits every context: practical, proportionate tools, following the Principles for Digital Development.'
    detail: The HDA recognizes that no single solution is appropriate for every context. Services, tools, methodologies, and products are designed to meet the specific needs, capacities, and realities of each operational environment. This principle promotes flexibility, proportionality, and user-centered design, ensuring that support remains practical, accessible, and effective across diverse humanitarian settings, following the [Principles for Digital Development](https://digitalprinciples.org/), and in particular reusing and improving existing solutions, designing with and for people, and building for sustainability.
