iMMAP · CartONG · HOT · Kobo

Four layers of expertise. One clear picture.

The Humanitarian Data Alliance brings together four organisations with deep expertise in data collection, open mapping, geospatial analysis and information management, so humanitarian actors get coordinated support across the crisis lifecycle.

What we do

One entry point to complementary expertise.

Instead of navigating four organisations, humanitarian actors reach aligned services that are designed to work together.

Request support

Data collection & management

Field-ready digital data collection, cleaning and management, with responsible-data safeguards built in.

Mapping & geospatial

Open, community-generated base maps, remote sensing and GIS analysis that show where needs are.

Information management & analysis

Turning raw data into products, dashboards and analysis that inform timely decisions.

Capacity strengthening

Training and accompaniment that builds lasting local expertise, leadership and ownership.

Across the crisis lifecycle

The picture builds, before, during and after a crisis.

  1. Phase 01

    Preparedness

    Baseline data, maps and trained local teams in place before a crisis hits.

  2. Phase 02

    Rapid response

    Coordinated surge support so responders get the right data, fast.

  3. Phase 03

    Sustained operations

    Reliable information management for protracted and forgotten crises.

  4. Phase 04

    Early recovery

    Handover to local actors, with tools and skills that stay.

What we commit to

Collaboration creates more value than any of us can achieve alone.

  1. Coordination over fragmentation

    Strengthen coordination and reduce fragmentation across the humanitarian data ecosystem.

  2. Expertise where it’s needed

    Make specialised technical expertise more accessible where it is needed most.

  3. Interoperable by design

    Develop interoperable, demand-driven and fit-for-purpose services.

  4. Responsible & open

    Promote responsible data stewardship and open knowledge.

  5. Local leadership

    Reinforce local expertise, leadership and long-term capacity.

  6. Practical innovation

    Pursue innovation that responds to real humanitarian challenges.

People will always come before data.

Every dataset, map, analysis and tool must ultimately serve a greater purpose: enabling faster, more informed and more accountable decisions for communities affected by crises.
