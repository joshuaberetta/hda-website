What went into G

  • Confluence sets the structure, navy, red and type. It was the clear first choice.
  • Fieldwork adds warm paper, contour textures and clear pastel colours, which reviewers said were easier to read.
  • Atlas adds the interactive layer stack (one layer per service) and building lifecycle. Signal adds monospace data labels.
  • Convergence II keeps the most-picked mark but makes it less “standard”: the pins swirl inward to a contour summit, borrowed from Contour.

How to review

Every page has a small review bar at the bottom. Use it to switch theme, page and logo without losing your place.

  1. Open a theme below. Each one has a home page, a style guide covering logo use, colour, type, components and graphics, and a collateral page with brochure, slides and event pieces.
  2. Try other logos with the Logo menu in the review bar. Every logo works in every theme, so any combination is valid.
  3. Pick your top three. For each favourite combination, click “Copy link” in the review bar and paste it into the review doc under your name, best first. The link records the theme, page, logo and any Contour studio settings. Comments are welcome too, e.g. “A’s layout with C’s colours”.

Website themes

Logo concepts

Each concept is built from four equal parts, one for each member, with no part given more weight. See the logos page for rationale, variants and co-branding.

Design principles

Taken from the collaboration documents, and applied to every option.

People before data

The MoU’s first value. Copy leads with outcomes for affected communities. Warm neutrals and plain language keep it human.

Alongside, not above

A light identity that sits next to the member brands. No member colour is “owned” by one organisation, and all four are always named.

Built from what we share

Navy (iMMAP, Kobo, CartONG), red (iMMAP, HOT) and teal (CartONG, Kobo) recur across our sites, so the palettes start there.

Data and maps, visibly

Dot maps, contours, relief, layers and tiles say “GIS and data” without stock photos.

Low bandwidth, high access

Static HTML, inline SVG graphics, WCAG AA contrast checks and no heavy imagery, so it works well on field connections.

Open and interoperable

Open-source fonts and a simple token system make it easy for any member’s team to reuse in slides, reports and social posts.
